# The first email to a professor

Professors get many generic "I'm interested in your research" emails and skip most of them. A short email that
shows the student read their work, says what the student can actually do, and asks one clear question gets
answered far more often.

## Rules

- **Short.** 120 to 180 words. No long life story.
- **Subject:** specific, for example "ASU sophomore interested in your sepsis prediction work".
- **One real detail** about their work, from their profile, lab site or a recent paper. Never invent one.
- **Only true claims** about the student, from the resume or what they told you. No inflated skills.
- **One clear ask:** a 15-minute meeting, or whether the lab takes undergrads this semester or next.
- **Plain and polite:** "Dr. <Last name>" or "Professor <Last name>", no slang, no emojis.
- **Resume** attached only if the student agrees; mention it in one line.
- Sign with the student's full name, major, year, and ASU email.
- Write in the student's voice. Show it to them and change anything they want before saving the draft.

## Shape

```text
Subject: ASU <year> in <major> interested in your <topic> research

Dear Dr. <Last name>,

I'm a <year> in <major> at ASU. I read about your work on <specific project or paper>, and
<one sentence on what caught their interest and why>.

<One or two sentences on relevant experience: a class project, a tool or method they used, a result.>

Is your lab taking undergraduate researchers this semester or next? I'd be glad to meet for
15 minutes at a time that works for you. My resume is attached.

Thank you,
<Full name>
<Major>, <year>
<ASU email>
```

## Follow-up (once, after about 10 days with no reply)

```text
Subject: Re: <original subject>

Dear Dr. <Last name>,

I wanted to follow up on my note below about undergraduate research in your lab. I'm still very
interested and happy to work around your schedule.

Thank you,
<Full name>
```

If there is still no reply after the follow-up, move on to the next match. Silence is common and not personal.
